\subsection{A two-frequency positive weight}
\label{ssec:fejer-two-frequency}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fejer-two-frequency}
The signed Liouville Fej\'er energy satisfies the unconditional bound
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{Q_\lambda(N)}N
       >\frac{1811}{2500}=0.7244.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(C=\limsup_{N\to\infty}Q_\lambda(N)/N\). It suffices to
consider \(C<\infty\). In
\(\mathcal H=L^2((0,1);\mathbb C^2)\), put
\[
 U_X(u)=(L(uX),L(X)-L(uX)),\quad
 w_s(u)=(u^s,1-u^s),\quad F(t)=e^{-t/2}U_{e^t}.
\]
Here \(L(X)=0\) for \(0<X<1\). For \(X=N+a\) with
\(0\le a<1\), the prefix and suffix formula gives
\[
 \|U_X\|_{\mathcal H}^2
    =\frac{NQ_\lambda(N)+a|L(N)|^2}{X},\qquad
 |L(N)|^2\le2Q_\lambda(N).
\]
Consequently \(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|F(t)\|_{\mathcal H}=\sqrt C\).
Partial summation and the substitution \(v=uX\) give
\[
 \int_0^\infty F(t)e^{-(s-1/2)t}\dd t
   =M(s)w_s,\qquad M(s)=\frac{\zeta(2s)}{s\zeta(s)}.
\]
The identity holds first for \(\R s>1\), and then for
\(\R s>1/2\) by holomorphy of the integral under the bound
on \(F\). Thus \(M(s)w_s\) has no pole in that open
half-plane, and its poles on the boundary have order at most one.

Let \(\rho_j=1/2+i\gamma_j\), \(j=1,2\), be the first two
positive critical zeros. They are simple. Put
\[
 A_j=\frac{\zeta(2\rho_j)}{\rho_j\zeta'(\rho_j)},\qquad
 a=-\frac1{\zeta(1/2)}>0,\qquad m=30.
\]
Choose real phases \(\theta_j\) satisfying
\(e^{i\theta_j}A_j=-|A_j|\), and take
\[
 P(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{2}
    \frac1{m+1}\left|\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}
             e^{i\ell(\gamma_jt-\theta_j)}\right|^2.
\]
This is a nonnegative trigonometric polynomial. Its frequencies
are \(n_1\gamma_1+n_2\gamma_2\), with \(-30\le n_1,n_2\le30\).
The accompanying ball-arithmetic certificate checks that all
\(3721\) frequency enclosures are pairwise disjoint. In
particular only \((n_1,n_2)=(0,0)\) has zero frequency, and
the constant coefficient of \(P\) is one. It follows that
\[
 \delta\int_0^\infty P(t)e^{-\delta t}\dd t\longrightarrow1.
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), one has
\(\|F(t)\|_{\mathcal H}\le\sqrt C+\varepsilon\) for all
sufficiently large \(t\). The contribution of a fixed bounded
interval vanishes after multiplication by \(\delta\).
Nonnegativity therefore gives
\[
 \limsup_{\delta\downarrow0}
   \left\|\delta\int_0^\infty
          F(t)P(t)e^{-\delta t}\dd t\right\|_{\mathcal H}
       \le\sqrt C.
\]

Outside the origin and the four fundamental frequencies,
the certificate verifies
\[
 \zeta\bigl(1/2+i(n_1\gamma_1+n_2\gamma_2)\bigr)\ne0.
\]
There are \(3716\) such tests. The vector Mellin identity shows
that their terms tend to zero in the last integral. The origin
contributes \(-a w_{1/2}\). Each pair \(\pm\gamma_j\)
contributes \(-c_j\R w_{\rho_j}\), where
\(c_j=60|A_j|/31\). Indeed the coefficient of
\(e^{i\gamma_jt}\) in \(P\) is
\((30/31)e^{-i\theta_j}\), and its Mellin argument is
\(1/2+\delta-i\gamma_j\). Pairing it with the negative
frequency gives \(2(30/31)\R(e^{i\theta_j}A_jw_{\rho_j})\).
Thus the vector limit exists and
\[
 C\ge\left\|a w_{1/2}
             +c_1\R w_{\rho_1}+c_2\R w_{\rho_2}
      \right\|_{\mathcal H}^{2}.
\]
The squared norm is computed using the exact identity
\begin{align*}
 \langle\R w_z,\R w_w\rangle_{\mathcal H}
   ={}&1-\R\frac1{1+z}-\R\frac1{1+w}\notag\\
      &+\R\frac1{1+z+w}
         +\R\frac1{1+z+\overline w}.
\end{align*}
The certificate uses \texttt{python-flint}~0.9.0 with
\(128\)-bit Arb/Acb arithmetic. It isolates the two simple
zeros, verifies that both derivative enclosures exclude zero,
proves strict separation of every frequency enclosure, and
evaluates all \(3716\) regular zeta values over those
enclosures. It obtains the squared-norm enclosure
\[
 0.72445959780354945892543005712377997
       \ \mathord{\pm}\ 7.55\cdot10^{-36},
\]
whose lower endpoint exceeds the exact rational \(1811/2500\).
The FLINT routines return rigorous enclosures, as documented
in the library's
\href{https://flintlib.org/doc/acb_dirichlet/index.html\#zeros-of-the-riemann-zeta-function}%
{zeta-zero reference}. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

The frequency checks concern this finite polynomial. A sequence
of weights yielding an unbounded lower bound would require
control of the additional integer combinations and their pole
contributions at every stage.
